# Chapter template

> _Last reviewed: YYYY-MM-DD — see the [freshness policy](./maintenance.md)._

Every SA AI chapter should help the reader make and defend an architecture decision.

## Learning objectives

Use three to five observable outcomes: *select, design, compare, measure, defend*. Avoid vague verbs such as *understand*.

## Decision in one sentence

State the decision the chapter equips the reader to make.

## Why it matters

Anchor the concept in the recurring Northstar case or another realistic scenario. Name the failure caused by getting it wrong.

## Mental model

Explain the durable, vendor-neutral model. Use one compact Mermaid diagram when relationships or sequence matter.

## Options and trade-offs

Compare at least two plausible options. Include quality, risk, latency, operability, and cost where relevant.

## Architecture walkthrough

Trace inputs, trust boundaries, data flow, failure behavior, observability, and human responsibility.

## Practical artifact

Produce a reusable SA artifact: decision matrix, C4 diagram, threat model, evaluation plan, cost model, ADR, or review checklist.

## Lab

Link a public starter and state the exact deliverable and acceptance criteria. A chapter without a code lab may use a decision lab.

## Check yourself

Ask three to five questions that require reasoning, not recall. At least one should apply the concept to an unfamiliar case.

## Further reading

Prefer primary documentation, standards, and papers. Record a last-reviewed date and avoid undated claims about fast-moving products.
